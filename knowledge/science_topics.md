## Orbital mechanics
### Kepler's laws
Kepler's first law says planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus. The second law says a line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times, so a planet moves fastest at closest approach. The third law says the square of the orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis: T^2 = 4 pi^2 a^3 / (G M).

### Orbital velocity and escape velocity
For a circular orbit of radius r around a mass M the orbital speed is v = sqrt(G M / r). The escape velocity from distance r is v = sqrt(2 G M / r), which is sqrt(2) times the circular orbital speed. From Earth's surface the escape velocity is about 11.2 km/s, and a low Earth orbit speed is about 7.8 km/s.

### Orbit shapes
An orbit's shape depends on its energy. A bound orbit is an ellipse (a circle is the special case of zero eccentricity). Exactly at escape speed the path is a parabola, and above escape speed it is a hyperbola. Eccentricity e between 0 and 1 means a closed ellipse. A satellite changes orbit by changing speed, for example a Hohmann transfer uses two engine burns to move between circular orbits.

## Astronomy
### Black holes
A black hole is a region of spacetime where gravity is so strong that nothing, not even light, can escape from inside the event horizon. The Schwarzschild radius r_s = 2 G M / c^2 gives the horizon size for a non-rotating black hole; for one solar mass it is about 2.95 km. Stellar black holes form when very massive stars collapse at the end of their lives.

### Stars
A star shines because nuclear fusion in its core turns hydrogen into helium and releases energy. The Sun is a main-sequence star about 4.6 billion years old. A star's life depends mostly on its mass: massive stars burn fast and end as supernovae leaving neutron stars or black holes, while Sun-like stars end as white dwarfs.

## Mechanics
### Newton's laws
The first law: an object keeps its state of motion unless a net force acts. The second law: net force equals mass times acceleration, F = m a. The third law: when one body pushes on another, the other pushes back with an equal and opposite force.

### Energy and momentum
Energy is conserved: it changes form but the total stays constant in an isolated system. Kinetic energy is 1/2 m v^2 and gravitational potential energy near Earth is m g h. Momentum p = m v is conserved in collisions when no external force acts.

## Electronics
### Ohm's law and circuits
Ohm's law says voltage equals current times resistance, V = I R. Power is P = V I. In a series circuit the same current flows through every part and resistances add. In a parallel circuit the voltage is the same across each branch and the reciprocals of resistance add.

### Capacitors
A capacitor stores charge Q = C V and energy E = 1/2 C V^2. In an RC circuit the time constant is tau = R C; after one time constant a charging capacitor reaches about 63 percent of the final voltage.

## Waves and light
### Waves
A wave carries energy without carrying matter along. Wave speed equals frequency times wavelength, v = f lambda. Sound is a longitudinal pressure wave needing a medium, about 343 m/s in air at 20 degrees Celsius; light is a transverse electromagnetic wave that can travel through vacuum at 299,792,458 m/s.

### Light and photons
Light behaves as both a wave and a stream of photons. A photon's energy is E = h f = h c / wavelength, so shorter wavelengths such as ultraviolet carry more energy per photon than longer wavelengths such as infrared.

## Thermodynamics
### Laws of thermodynamics
The zeroth law defines temperature equality. The first law says energy is conserved: the change in internal energy equals heat added minus work done by the system. The second law says the entropy of an isolated system never decreases, so heat flows from hot to cold on its own. The third law says absolute zero cannot be reached in a finite number of steps.

### Ideal gas
The ideal gas law is P V = n R T with R = 8.314 J per mole kelvin. It describes gases well at low pressure and high temperature. Temperature must be in kelvin: K = degrees Celsius + 273.15.

## Chemistry
### Atoms and the periodic table
An atom has a nucleus of protons and neutrons surrounded by electrons. The number of protons (atomic number) decides the element. Elements in the same column of the periodic table have similar chemistry because they have the same number of outer electrons.

### Moles and concentration
One mole is 6.02214076e23 particles. The number of moles equals mass divided by molar mass, n = m / M. Molarity is moles of solute per litre of solution, c = n / V.

## Relativity and quantum physics
### Special relativity
Special relativity rests on two ideas: the laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames, and the speed of light in vacuum is the same for all observers. Consequences include time dilation, length contraction and E = m c^2.

### Quantum basics
In quantum mechanics energy comes in discrete amounts (quanta) and particles have wave-like behaviour. The Heisenberg uncertainty principle says position and momentum cannot both be known exactly: delta x times delta p is at least h divided by 4 pi.
